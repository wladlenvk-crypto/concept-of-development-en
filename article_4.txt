ARTICLE 4
Unlearned Lessons of Dialectics: From Whom and How to Learn to Think
Dialectically
Abstract
This article is dedicated to the lessons of dialectics from Vladimir I. Lenin — an
outstanding national thinker to whom the ability of clear dialectical thinking was
generously gifted by nature. Any work that came from Lenin's pen is imbued
with the spirit of dialectics.
Any Lenin's thought is a chain or series of conceptual forms maximally filled with
objective content. Any of his theoretical work is a model of masterfully balanced
sliding of thought as if on the "razor's edge", the opposite sides-facets of which
symbolize the confrontation of two non-identical opposing sides-components of
reality.
The aim of this article is to draw the attention primarily of the young reader
precisely to these qualities of Vladimir I. Lenin's theoretical works.
In the course of familiarizing oneself with Lenin's theoretical heritage, the
circumstance that the research approach he used remains unclaimed to this day,
and Lenin's lessons of dialectics, accordingly, remain unlearned, catches the eye.
Since at the foundation of any certain form of reality (object, process,
phenomenon) lie two highly abstract and non-identical, yet at the same time
objective and interrelated constituent sides-components "content" and "form",
the content-form relations (conversions, transformations) come to the fore and
become the subject of close attention of dialectics.
Considering Lenin's theoretical works through the prism of content-form relations
and identifying on this basis the general regularities of the construction and
presentation of material makes it possible to speak about the possibility of
creating a single universal methodology for conducting effective research at any
level and in any field of reality.
Introduction

As an epigraph to this article, we would like to take words by Vladimir I. Lenin
from his work "On the Road", written in 1909: "...this stage must be outgrown;
the new circumstances of the moment require new forms of struggle..." [8, p.
540].
The cited quote, in our view, vividly expresses not only the character of a
remarkable living and flexible personality but also the essence of the entire life
and revolutionary activity of V.I. Lenin: keenly and relentlessly tracking the
situation, accurately grasping its changes, and being constantly ready to respond
to these changes with the most adequate actions — new forms of struggle.
In this article, we will examine and analyze the greatness and scale of Lenin's
figure from the standpoint of his innate ability: to be a dialectician. Discarding
all fictional, doubtful and secondary elements that were thrust into the
foreground during the "murky" 1990s — years of merciless reprisals against
Soviet power and the USSR — we will focus mainly on this aspect of Lenin's
activities and personality in our research.
A dialectical mindset is that indispensable trait of Lenin's individuality which, in
our opinion, has not yet received due coverage in fiction and journalism. All
other attributes of genius are already derivatives of such outstanding features of
his nature: adherence to a scientific approach in studying any issues; striving for
objective and impartial presentation of conclusions and results obtained through
observations and studies, etc. It was precisely Lenin's natural "immersion" in the
mysterious medium of dialectics that provoked fierce hostility and rejection
towards him from his enemies and opponents who, lacking this natural gift,
could boast of nothing more than inventing plots "out of their heads" and
presenting scholastic twists, assumptions, fables and fairy tales.
Another reason for pathological hatred and animosity toward Lenin's personality
lies in the inability of his opponents to achieve a penetrating, Lenin-like insight
into the essence and order of things; and this unattainable depth of
understanding actual relations remains the main irritant for his opponents even
now.
What is the strength of Lenin, if even his most die-hard detractors and
adversaries nevertheless recognize his influence and authority on a global scale?
First of all, in our view, it lies in his philosophical significance and specifically in
mastery of dialectics: when foresight is the result of a talent of an individual to
see the development of reality through the prism of interaction between directly
opposing sides of this (reality). He was what many ordinary and extraordinary
philosophizing individuals secretly desire and dream of becoming, realizing
however that they fall short, do not measure up, and cannot become anyone
similar — because some extremely important constituent elements are missing
in the multi-layered structure of their own "I".
It is difficult to find a modern public leader who stands at least level with (on the
same step) Lenin — an encyclopedic scholar and philosopher of profound depth,

a brilliant polemicist, consistent and fearless revolutionary, prominent politician
and outstanding statesman.
Possession of the invaluable gift of a born dialectician allowed Lenin to handle
events and circumstances with exquisite precision, balancing on the crest of the
wave.
The accuracy and precision with which historical figures of a dialectical "leaven"
walk along the "razor's edge", balancing between Scylla and Charybdis in
interpreting the questions under study, evokes admiration and envy in the best
sense of the word. Through them (and via them), dialectics charts its way most
effectively and optimally. It is precisely through them and their voices that
dialectically developing nature speaks to us.
It is regrettable, of course, that the beauty and perfection of thought, say, of
Marx or Engels can be appreciated by most of us only thanks to the efforts of
translators. Undoubtedly, those translators of the time were highly qualified
specialists and quite exceptional people, but as someone aptly noted, reading
classics in translation is like taking a shower while wearing a coat. And so much
the better that the beauty, clarity and precision of Lenin's thought can, thank
god, be known and evaluated without the interference of intermediary
translators.
All people (especially outstanding ones) are to one degree or another
spontaneous dialecticians. And it does not matter what craft (of mental or
physical labor) they possess. Dialectics, ingrained in the flesh and blood of every
person, occasionally orients him/her towards certain paradoxical (it seems, from
the point of view of common sense) solutions, thanks to which he/she achieves
remarkable results and therefore becomes famous among the general public. We
refer here, for example, to memoirs and recollections of celebrated personalities:
Marshal Zhukov, pilots Pokryshkin and Kozhedub, aircraft engine designer
Mikulin, director Eisenstein, cardiac surgeon Amosov, weightlifter Vlasov and
many others whose innate art of paradoxical dialectical thinking helped them
"squeeze through" literally through the eye of a needle and win unpredictable
victories, make discoveries, achieve unexpected and unforeseen success in
seemingly unfavorable situations according to everyday consciousness. But Lenin
was by no means a spontaneous latent dialectician, whom there are countless
numbers of on planet Earth; rather, like Hegel, Marx and Engels, he undoubtedly
felt and realized himself as a dialectician and acted accordingly to the dialectical
nature of the world and the set of tools provided by this nature: not inventing
tools out of one's head, but discovering them using one's head [16, p. 148].
Accordingly, any conclusion drawn by Lenin is not arbitrariness or fabrication,
but a sober judgment based on careful study and meticulous analysis of the
established situation with identification of all the "actors and performers".
As mentioned above, through thinkers such as Marx, Hegel, Engels, Lenin,
nature itself (the surrounding world, developing reality) talks to us in the
language of pure dialectics. And our Vladimir Ilyich looks in this row of

With the expectation that this article may provide some support in mastering the

Ultimately, what is important to know when studying various forms of reality:
object, process, phenomenon, etc.? It is essential to identify and track the logic,
historicism, trend, patterns, direction (i.e., the contentful side) of the
development of these forms of manifestation and existence of reality, for which it
turns out to be absolutely necessary to observe how transformation
(transmutation, conversion) of one form of reality into another takes place, and
furthermore, on this basis, try to forecast such transformations (transformations,
conversions), anticipating not only the change of existing forms but also the
emergence of new forms. This constitutes the subject matter of dialectics.
Ultimately, it comes down to studying the surrounding world (nature, man,
society) from the position of content-form transformations, transmutations,
conversions [2, 3, 7, 18].

Every serious researcher, embarking on research work, proceeds from the
premise that reality (the surrounding world, nature) is always at least dual-sided
(i.e., double-faceted). Any real specific object, process or phenomenon in its
most general abstract form represents a relation of two principles (sides,
constituents, components, "opponents" etc.): {content/form}. Consequently,
any concrete form of reality (implying: object, process, phenomenon) is
determined by the correlation of these noted sides-constituents-components
within each of their specific relations. The development of forms of reality itself
represents changes in the correlations of these constituent-components within
their relations. In this connection it becomes obvious that forms of reality
defined by the aforementioned ratios can be either more or less Contentful (due
to different shares of content and formal components in their relations). The
overall trend of development and change of forms of reality — as human
observations throughout human history show — is such that during their (forms')
development there occurs a shift in the ratio of components (in their relations)
towards the numerator of the relation (if we consider such a relation as a
mathematical fraction), i.e., toward the Contentful component of the relation
(meaning the fundamental orientation toward the general movementdevelopment of forms of reality in the direction of increasing
their Contentfulness, and ultimately achieving ultimate highest Contentfulness)
[5, 7]. This setting-orientation indicating the direction of variability of all forms is
called the universal principle of self-development (self-organization) of reality,
formulated as follows: the development of the content of forms of reality takes
place in the direction of exhaustive expression of developing content by the very
same developing content of these forms [3, 5, 7, 18].
Applied to the surrounding world, this formulation means roughly the following:
everything that exists and develops has the direction of its development and
existence expressed (i.e., encompassed, absorbed, assimilated, comprehended,
etc.) by itself existing and developing. As a visual example in this case, the
image of a "self-winding" three-dimensional spiral seems most appropriate [2,
7]. And such an orientation or tendency of change-movement-development
belongs to all developing forms of reality without exception.
For greater illustrativeness of what was just said, note that with regard to such a
level (area) of developing reality as, say, man and society, this universal
principle drives forward not only positive (from the standpoint of human
consciousness) morally ethical and humanitarian aspirations of man but also
negative ones (again, in terms of human consciousness) — his thoughts,
motives, secret impulses and "sinful" inclinations — such as arrogance and
condescension, feelings of exclusivity and superiority over one's neighbour, envy
and vanity, etc., etc., Everyone intends and strains to present himself and
appear as the most significant, competent, respectable, successful, etc., in short
— the most contentful, which is also determined and conditioned by the same
universal objective striving of any forms for possession of the
highest contentfulness. After all, how sweet and tempting it is to participate in
some kind of political hangout and be considered in this world an influential
person — a disposer of human destinies!

Thus, in light of the dialectical concept of development presented here,
objective reality represents motion and succession of its forms in the direction
of possessing the highest contentfulness (i.e., maximally achievable content),
and all processes of development and cognition of reality should be studied and
viewed through the prism of content-form transitions, transformations,
transmutations of unitary (i.e., strictly defined) directionality.
A specifically directed variability of the abstract relation {content/form} is
conditioned, on the one hand, by the unity and, on the other hand, non-identity
(non-sameness) of its components, due to which both components are in a state
of interaction and inexhaustible movement towards convergence with each other
and acquisition of equilibrium (balance) or mutual expressibility by each other
(which means: becoming maximally contentful for form and defining its perfect
form for content). The presence in one integral relation of two components with
different types of activity generates a whirlwind, vortex, circulation, "selfwinding" spiral, i.e., a specific type of change (movement, development) of both
components of this relation and the relation as a whole.
The famous saying of Heraclitus about everything flowing and changing still left
open the question of where exactly everything flows and changes — clear ideas
were lacking until now. Only today does the opportunity arise to answer this
question more or less sensibly; today we can meaningfully define the vector of
this universal movement: everywhere and always there is total multi-level
movement towards reaching the highest level of contentfulness; everything
is pervaded by the striving and covered by movement towards possession of the
highest contentfulness. This universal movement vector determines causality,
meanings and goals: life, development, evolution, history, etc., i.e., the
existence of anything whatsoever, in whatever forms.
The algorithm of development — common and universal for all levels-forms of
reality, from photon to the most elaborate conceptual schemes and constructions
in individual minds: new content will always strive to acquire a new form, while
forms seek to be filled, enriched with new content. Hence — a vortex-like motion
of everything. By this conception, only the typical, standard character of the
course and transformation of things at all levels is pointed out: the universal
generalized relation {content/form} is embodied on each specific level in its own
concrete form and consistently "proceeds" through levels towards replacing
relations with increasingly more contentful ones.
The movement or striving to achieve the highest contentfulness (as a single
goal for all changing forms of reality) conditions the fiercest rivalry and
competition between such moving and developing forms of reality. The inevitable
competition and rivalry arising from this movement (movement toward a
common finish line) manifests itself as confrontation, opposition and
interaction of real objects, processes and phenomena at different stages, in
different areas and at different levels of their existence-movement-development.

Being an entirely abstract construct, the relation {content/form} is
fundamentally not manifestable in its most general form or image, but it
nevertheless constitutes the core or very essence of any and every real concrete
form (object, process, phenomenon, etc.).
The noted duality of constituents-components of real forms-relations with a very
strict and definite directionality of content-form transformations during the
interaction of these constituents-components explains the moment-to-moment
uniqueness of the emerging world (Universe, nature, life, etc.) as a whole, as
well as the unique and unprecedented diversity of existing and nascent
forms as individual elements of this world.
The abstract relation {content/form} with quite unequivocal directionality of
change-movement-development of its components or constituents, just like the
entire relation as a whole, is something that exists originally, everywhere,
objectively and independently of any particular concreteness; it is what pervades
all existing and emerging concrete forms-levels-relations of surrounding reality
due to its omnipresence. It is precisely the content-form transformations
described above (i.e., changes of components and their relations in a strictly
defined direction — towards achieving the highest contentfulness) that underlie
the development of forms of reality, which are themselves only a concrete
embodiment and incarnation of the model presented above (i.e., the abstract
relation {content/form}). And it is exactly this initial model, owing to its
foundational and universality, that is most acceptable and effective for deep
study, research and penetration into the essence of the surrounding world
(developing reality) at the current stage of its development.
This investigative approach demonstrates its effectiveness through examples of

problematic and "tricky" [5, 6].
Why do content-form relations suddenly come to the fore, become the core and
subject of examination and study of dialectics? Because the very fact and the
corresponding irrefutable assertion that there does not exist a single real object,
process or phenomenon devoid of content and/or formal sides-constituents
inevitably leads to the conclusion that content and form are those two
"principles" (two main key components, constituents, "opponents", etc.) which,

interacting (opposing and simultaneously complementing each other) within the
unified integral relation {content/form}, create ("generate") and reproduce socalled objective reality, i.e., the entire surrounding world (including ourselves)
with all the diversity and uniqueness of its forms.
According to the textbook definition, dialectics is "the teaching about the most
general lawful connections and becoming, development of being and cognition,
and the method of creatively cognizing thinking based on this teaching... The
fundamental principles of dialectics, constituting its backbone, are universal
connection, becoming and development, which are conceptualized using the
entire historically established system of categories and laws" [15, p. 154].
The priority of the categories {content/form} over all others is obvious and
confirmed by the circumstance that in the foundation of all other categories and
concepts (just as of the actual objects, processes, phenomena of the physicalobjective level of reality denoted by these categories and concepts), one can
easily discover ubiquitous content-form beginnings-constituents-components.
It goes without saying and is undeniable that no one will ever be able to
encompass (i.e., comprehend) the entire volume of connections and the total
sum of changes occurring in all elements of the surrounding world (starting from
photons, including the abyss of atoms, molecules, living cells, etc., and ending
again with bizarre ideas and fantasies in the heads of individual human
individuals), but grasping and covering the trend or patterns of such changes
with human thought (from the standpoint of human thought or consciousness
itself) still turns out to be possible.
Here is what Lenin writes on this matter in his book “Materialism and
Empiriocriticism”, referring to just a relatively small segment of reality — the
global economy: "Each separate producer in the world economy is aware that he
introduces a certain change in production technique, each owner is aware that
he exchanges some products for others, but these producers and owners do not
realize that they thereby alter social being. No 70 Marxs could have covered
the sum of all these changes in all their ramifications in capitalist world
economy. At best, the laws of these changes have been discovered, the main
and basic objective logic of these changes and their historical development has
been shown... From the fact that you live and manage your affairs, give birth to
children and produce goods, exchange them, an objectively necessary chain of
events emerges, a chain of development independent of your public
consciousness, never fully comprehended by it. The highest task of
humanity is to grasp this objective logic of economic evolution
(evolution of social being) in general and basic terms in order to adapt
one's public consciousness to it as clearly, distinctly and critically as
possible..." [11, pp. 317–318].

1. In his book “What Is to Be Done? Burning Questions of Our Movement” [8,
pp. 80–236], published in March 1902, Lenin presented an overview of the initial
stage of the revolutionary movement in Russia. The book was written during the

acute struggle for the creation of a revolutionary Marxist party of the Russian
working class and played an outstanding role in the formation of the party.
In this work, Lenin identifies three stages and accordingly — three periods in the
development of the revolutionary movement in Russia.
He describes the first stage of 1884–1894 as the embryonic development of
social democracy carried out separately from the labor movement.
Regarding the second stage of 1894–1898: "social-democracy appears on earth
as a public movement, as an upsurge of popular masses, as a political party" [8,
p. 227]. Alongside and instead of "going into the people", there began a flow of
intelligentsia toward workers (as the most active force-component of the future
revolutionary movement) and widespread fascination among workers with
strikes. However, this same strike struggle for the sake of increasing its own
efficiency forced them to learn, read illegal literature, as well as diligently engage
with issues of legal populism without forgetting at the same time about Marxist
theory and the task of overthrowing autocracy. "The establishment of the party
in the spring of 1898 was the most vivid and at the same time last act of the
Social Democrats of this stripe" [8, p. 228].
The third stage finally replaces the second one starting in 1898. It is a period of
confusion, vacillation, disintegration. The leaders of the movement were afraid to
move forward (to political struggle), yielding to the strength of the spontaneous
upsurge of the popular masses. The slogan "class struggle" did not push towards
ever broader, more energetic activity but served as a means of appeasement:
after all, everyone knows that "economic struggle is inextricably linked with
political". The idea of the party did not serve as a call to create a combat
organization of revolutionaries but justified some kind of "revolutionary
bureaucracy", childish playing with "democratic" forms. And here, in this
connection, the author, like a true scholar drawing conclusions only based on
facts (without fabrications or fantasies), having difficulty saying when the third
stage will end and the fourth begin, nevertheless expresses firm confidence that
the fourth stage will lead to strengthening militant Marxism; that the rear-guard
of conciliators and deserters will be replaced by the actual vanguard of the most
revolutionary class (proletariat), which will manage to overcome uncertainty,
hesitation and lack of confidence in their own forces. And then (in terms of
calling for such a change and summarizing what has been said), it becomes
possible to give a concise and exhaustive answer to the question "what is to be
done?" — liquidate the third stage (period of confusion, vacillations, hesitations
and lack of confidence in one's own forces) [8, p. 229].

potential sympathizers. As a result of this struggle, the idea emerged of
establishing and organizing a monolithic nucleus of the labor movement — the
Bolshevik Party — in opposition to amateurishness and circle-mindedness
characteristic of the social-democratic movement. Without consolidation around
a single center, without subordination to decisions of this center (that is, without

iron discipline), the future conquests of the Great October would have simply
been unthinkable and impossible. Precisely from the standpoint of the dialectical
approach (from beginning to end), Lenin conducts his painstaking analysis of the
accompanying events of this struggle. For the proletariat (and any public
association alike) there is no other weapon in the struggle for power
except organization (organized effort) — writes Lenin. The proletariat can
become an invincible force only because the ideological unity of its theoretical
principles (first of all, Marxist theory) is secured by material unity of organization
uniting millions of workers into a single army (of the working class). Before this
army, the decrepit power of international capital will not stand. This army will
draw closer and tighter ranks despite any zigzags and steps backward [8, p.
413].
In the course of detailed study of the background of this issue, Lenin draws
attention to the fact that every development indeed proceeds along a dialectical
path of resolving emerging contradictions: minority becomes majority, majority
becomes minority, each side moves from defense to attack and from attack to
defense; the original point of ideological struggle is "negated", but then begins
"negation of negation" and the struggling sides return to the original point of
purely ideological struggle, but now this "thesis" is enriched by all the results of
the "antithesis" and turns into a higher "synthesis"; when an isolated random
error has grown into a quasi-system of conciliatory views on the organizational
issue, and the erroneousness of such an approach becomes increasingly
apparent. "In short, not only oats grow according to Hegel, but Russian socialdemocrats fight among themselves too according to Hegel," concludes Vladimir
Ilyich [8, p. 410].

3. The article "The Beginning of the Revolution in Russia" [8, pp. 422–
424] was written by Lenin in January 1905 — almost immediately after the
events of Bloody Sunday in St. Petersburg. Judging by the fact that the
spring was slowly compressing ("the tsarist government deliberately gave
relatively unhindered development to strike movements and the beginnings
of broad demonstrations"), it was not difficult to assume that the
government acted with the intention of driving things toward the use of
military force [8, p. 422]. As a result: thousands killed and wounded. The
Rubicon has been crossed: the compressed-to-the-limit spring had to release
its accumulated energy — the proletariat rose up and began to arm itself.
And immediately the entire palette of revolutionary appeals, slogans and
actions changes. The rebellious Petrograd workers put forward demands for
immediate convocation of a constituent assembly based on universal, direct,
equal and secret suffrage, as well as for the immediate overthrow of the
government; at the same time, "arming the people becomes one of the
nearest tasks of the revolutionary moment." Events develop with astonishing
speed: "the revolutionary education of the proletariat advanced in one day

more than it could have done in months and years of gray, humdrum,
downtrodden life". Immediate arming of workers and all citizens generally (it
was recognized and pointed out that revolutionaries of bourgeois democracy
could be used for the overthrow of Tsarism), "preparation and organization of
revolutionary forces for the destruction of governmental authorities and
institutions — this is the practical basis upon which all and any
revolutionaries can and should combine for a common blow" [8, p. 424].
[It should be noted here that there are no Councils of Workers', Soldiers' or

on the other side — autocracy with its coercive institutions].
And no matter how the present uprising ends in St. Petersburg itself, it will

postpone the hour of reckoning, but postponement will only make the next
step of the revolutionary onslaught even grander [8, p. 423].
[This example clearly demonstrates how V.I. Lenin masterfully formulated

(movement, development) of the active component and the whole relation;
forecasting the emergence, accordingly, of new contentful forms as a result
of this change-movement.

4. The article "Marxism and Revisionism" [8, pp. 36–43] was written in
March-April 1908. Describing the initial starting position, Lenin draws attention to
the alignment of forces (i.e., lists interacting components) in the course of the
struggle unfolding in the theoretical sphere. In this sphere, newly emerged
natural-historical theories in the mid-19th century, touching old theological
prejudices, caused and continue to cause the fiercest opposition and struggle [8,
p. 36]. Bourgeois scientific thought and philosophy do not want to hear anything
about Marxism, declaring it refuted and destroyed [how wonderfully "fresh" and

recognizable is such an attitude toward Marxism even today!]. The increasing
role of Marxism and consolidation of its ideas among the working class inevitably
provokes sharpening bourgeois attacks against Marxism, which after each
"destruction" by official science becomes stronger, tougher and livelier. Having
defeated and outplayed many hostile external currents over the past fifty years,
today Marxism faces opposing currents requiring its correction within Marxism
itself. Pre-Marxian socialism remains strong in Russia and sometimes revives.
Generally speaking, pre-Marxian socialism has been broken, but it continues
fighting not on its own independent ground but on common Marxist ground — as
revisionism [trying to legalize and prove viability of a mixture of "a bulldog with
a rhinoceros"]. Essentially, however, all this petty-bourgeois revision of Marxism
(revision of small shopkeepers) always boils down to negation or diminution of
the factor of class struggle and channeling class struggle into the stream of
"class cooperation" [8, pp. 38–39].
What accounts for the inevitability of periodic revival of revisionism and its roots
under capitalism? It lies in the fact that in any capitalist country there are always
broad layers of petty bourgeoisie, small proprietors alongside the proletariat.
Capitalism was born and constantly reborn (regenerated) from small production
[8, p. 42]! A whole series of "middle strata" [by analogy with some "middle
class", using modern language] is inevitably created again and again by
capitalism due to its inner necessity (services of individual craftsmen, homebased work, small workshops serving large industry, etc.). When they go
bankrupt, these small producers are inevitably thrown back into the ranks of the
proletariat, "diluting" their views and beliefs with petty-bourgeois worldview,
their ideology (which, apparently, requires constant work on retraining and reeducation). This is completely natural and will be so for a long time while
antagonistic classes exist. And ideological struggle of revolutionary Marxism with
revisionism is merely the prelude to great revolutionary battles of the proletariat
until complete victory of its cause despite all vacillations and weaknesses of the
petit bourgeoisie [8, p. 43].
Bypassing superficial phenomena (effects), Lenin constantly brings to light the
most essential, profound sides-components consisting in actual relations of
interaction and confrontation. Thus, contrary to the widespread opinion among
revisionists, he states that growing parliamentarism and education of the
proletariat do not weaken crises and eliminate revolutions but, on the contrary,
contribute to intensifying civil war during these crises and revolutions [8, p. 41].
They (parliamentarism and education) help enlighten and organize ever larger
masses of population correspondingly to the increase ("piling up") of substantive
(informational) accumulations. This constitutes the internal dialectics of
parliamentarism and bourgeois democratism, subordinate following of which
expresses itself in even more acute than before resolution of disputes and
contradictions through mass violence.
[Lenin is a master at identifying dialectically confronting components (i.e., the

The reason for writing the January 1909 article "On the Road" [8, pp. 537–544]
was the organizational and ideo-political crisis in the Social-Democratic Party of
Russia. The background of the crisis lay in the urgent need to cleanse the
"workers' party of wavering, intellectual and petty-bourgeois elements who had
joined the labor movement mainly hoping for the imminent triumph of the
bourgeois-democratic revolution and who could not hold out during the reaction"
[8, p. 538]. The instability of these "elements" affected both theory, tactics and
organizational policy of the party. Conscious workers took charge of running
party organizations and leading them. The situation required convening the AllRussian Conference of the RSDLP, where answers were actually formulated
regarding what path the working-class party should choose in the difficult
current period.
The content of this answer consisted of the following decisions and developments
of the conference: "Marxist analysis of the current relationship between classes
and new policies of Tsarism"; indication of the immediate goal of struggle set by
the Social-Democratic Party; "assessment of lessons learned from the revolution
concerning the correctness of revolutionary-social democratic tactics; clarification
of causes of the party's crisis and pointing to the role of the proletarian element
of the party in combating it; resolving the issue of the ratio of illegal and legal
organization"; recognition of the need to use the Duma tribune and development
of precise guiding instructions for the social-democratic faction in connection with
direct criticism of its errors [8, p. 538].
Autocracy [instead of the term "autocracy", one can use today's equivalents
(terms closest and similar in meaning are): authoritarianism, autocracy, tyranny,
despotism] still acts as the main enemy of the proletariat and all democracy, but
it would be a mistake to think that it remains unchanged, does not change or
mimic. Today "autocracy attempts to take upon itself the solution of objectively
necessary tasks of the bourgeois revolution — creation of popular representation

truly governing affairs of bourgeois society, and cleaning up medieval, tangled
and obsolete agrarian relations in the village; but precisely the practical result of
these new steps of autocracy turns out so far equal to zero, and this only makes
even clearer the necessity of other forces and means for solving the historical
task" [8, pp. 539–540].
New conditions of the moment require new forms of struggle: using the Duma
tribune appears an unconditional necessity; long-term work on education and
organization of masses of the proletariat is moved to the forefront; combining
illegal and legal organization presents special challenges to the party;
popularization and explanation of the experience of the revolution discredited by
liberals and liquidator intellectuals are needed both theoretically and practically.
But the tactical line of the party, which must manage to account for new
conditions in methods and tools of struggle, remains unchanged. The correctness
of revolutionary-social democratic tactics has been confirmed by the experience
of mass struggle of 1905–1907. The defeat of the revolution at the end of this
first campaign revealed not incorrectness of goals, not utopian nature of
objectives, not erroneousness of means and approaches, but insufficient
preparedness of forces, insufficient depth and breadth of the revolutionary crisis.
Let liberals and bewildered intelligentsia fall into despair after the first massive
battle for freedom. Conscious proletariat will respond to them that great tasks of
revolutions were solved only when advanced classes repeated their onslaught
more than once and achieved victory, having been taught by the experience of
defeats. Defeated armies learn well. Revolutionary classes of Russia have been
defeated in the first campaign, but the revolutionary situation persists. [Lenin's

admiration!!!]. In new forms and another way, the revolutionary crisis looms

concrete goals — should be carried out, and the more successfully it is done, the
surer the victory in the new struggle will be. And the same proletariat will now
be able to patiently and steadfastly carry out the work of educating and
preparing new cadres of a mightier revolutionary force.
[It concerns periodic evaluation of the correlation of interacting components in

§ 2. Works of the immediate pre-revolutionary period

of the October coup).
1. In the book "On the Right of Nations to Self-Determination" [8, pp.
569-621], written in May 1914, Lenin analyzes in detail the specifics of

Preliminarily, it should be said that the "right of nations to selfdetermination", regarding the legitimacy and expediency of whose
proclamation and protection heated debates do not cease even in our days, is
not a figment of V.I. Lenin's imagination or an invention of the Bolsheviks,
but the result of discussions and decisions of the International congresses,
and in particular the "London International Congress (of socialist workers'
parties and trade unions) of 1896". The defense of this right and its inclusion
in the program of the Social-Democratic Party of Russia was justified by
Lenin by referring to the corresponding stage of the national liberation
movement experienced by Russia. By analogy with the support by Marxists
under similar historical conditions of the said right in Western Europe, Lenin,
focusing on similar liberation processes in Eastern Europe and Asia (including
Russia), but occurring with some delay (at least by half a century), concludes
on the necessity of advancing the same demand at the stage of revolutionary
transformations in Russia as well.
To convey the logic of Lenin's thought, let us provide a chain of characteristic
quotes from this work.
"An unconditional requirement of Marxist theory in the analysis of any social
question whatsoever is to place it within definite historical limits, and then, if
the question concerns one country (for example, a national program for a
given country), to take into account the concrete specifics distinguishing this
country from others within the limits of one and the same historical epoch"
[8, p. 574].
"The comparison of the political and economic development of different
countries, as well as their Marxist programs, is of enormous significance from
the standpoint of Marxism, for both the general capitalist nature of modern
states and the general law of their development are indisputable. But such a
comparison must be made skillfully. The ABC condition here is to clarify
the question of whether the historical epochs of development of the
compared countries are comparable" [8, p. 578].
"In Western, continental Europe, the era of bourgeois-democratic revolutions
covers a rather definite time span, approximately from 1789 to 1871. It was
precisely this era that was the era of national movements and the creation of
national states. Upon the completion of this era, Western Europe turned into
an established system of bourgeois states, as a general rule, at the same
time nationally unified states. Therefore, to now seek the right to selfdetermination in the programs of West European socialists means not to
understand the ABC of Marxism.
In Eastern Europe and Asia, the era of bourgeois-democratic revolutions only
began in 1905. ...And in this chain of events only a blind man can fail to see
the awakening of a whole series of bourgeois-democratic national
movements, aspirations for the creation of nationally independent and

nationally unified states. It is precisely because and only because
Russia, together with neighboring countries, is experiencing this era
that we need a point on the right of nations to self-determination in
our program" [8, pp. 578-579].
"Thus, it is the historical concrete specifics of the national question in
Russia that give us a special urgency to the recognition of the right
of nations to self-determination in the epoch we are experiencing" [8,
p. 581].
"The bourgeoisie, which naturally acts as the hegemon (leader) of any
national movement at its beginning, calls the support of all national
aspirations a practical matter. But the policy of the proletariat in the national
question (as in other questions) only supports the bourgeoisie in a certain
direction, but never coincides with its policy. ...Every bourgeoisie wants in

this is called 'practical'. The proletariat is against any privileges, against
any exclusivity" [8, p. 582].
"The bourgeoisie always places its national demands in the foreground. It

interests of the class struggle. Theoretically, one cannot guarantee in

of the right to self-determination, guaranteeing nothing to any nation,

democratic of possible solutions; the proletariat needs only these guarantees,

farms and individual states is impossible, and therefore under capitalism
there are no other means of restoring from time to time the disturbed
equilibrium and eliminating disproportions than crises in industry and
wars in politics. War is not in contradiction with the foundations of private
property, but is a consequence of the direct and inevitable development of

these foundations (i.e. the need for periodic revision and redistribution of the
accumulations of private property).
Thus, based on what has been said on the modern economic basis, i.e. under
capitalism, the United States of Europe would mean the organization of

give rise to an incorrect interpretation of the question in the impossibility of

The unevenness of economic and political development is an absolute law of
capitalism. It follows from this that the victory of socialism is possible initially

less long, persistent struggle of socialist republics with backward states. By
virtue of these considerations, a conclusion is made about the incorrectness
of the slogan of the United States of Europe at this particular historical
moment [8, p. 636], since it will be expressed in the postponement of the
beginning of the socialist revolution.
[Contentful essence of the socialist revolution, as noted in the article, is

With regard to social transformations in Russia, the ultimately accomplished
socialist revolution with an aim at building a classless society is just an initial

universal development as a whole).
It is the striving to achieve the highest contentfulness by the forms-carriers

5. The article "The Military Program of the Proletarian Revolution" [8,
pp. 751-761] was written in German in September 1916, and was printed
only a year later. Then it was intended for Swiss, Swedish and Norwegian left
Social-Democrats. The publication was preceded by the following foreword:
"Nowadays, when Lenin is one of those figures of the Russian revolution who
are most often spoken about, the following article of this old iron
revolutionary, outlining a significant part of his political program, is of
particular interest" [8, p. 834]. In the Russian language in Russia, this work
was first published only in 1929.
Structurally and compositionally, the article is built according to the same very
typical stencil: analysis of the current situation (state of affairs); identification of
the interacting sides in their relation, both old and new (transformed and
developed); determination of the active, leading, leading component in these
relations, especially in the new one; forecasting the direction of variability of
both the components-constituents themselves in this new relation and the
relation as a whole.
The appearance of this article was caused by the circumstance that along with
the justified and fair opposition to the false slogan of the social-chauvinists about
the "defense of the fatherland" in the ongoing imperialist war, voices began to be
heard in favor of replacing the old point of the Social-Democratic program
"arming of the people" (formation of a "militia") with the new point
"disarmament".
"The main argument is that the demand for disarmament is the most consistent
expression of the struggle against all militarism and against any war." But in this
argument lies the main fallacy of the supporters of disarmament: socialists,

without ceasing to be socialists, cannot be against any war, and in particular a
revolutionary war, a national liberation war, a civil war (as well as the so-called
"defensive" wars of the victorious proletariat against the bourgeoisie of other
countries) [8, pp. 751-752]. And from a scientific point of view, it would be
completely incorrect and unrevolutionary to bypass or obscure the most
important and most difficult thing: the necessity of suppressing the resistance of
the bourgeoisie during the transition to socialism. And in wars of this kind, to
recognize "defense of the fatherland" means to consider these wars "just" and
corresponding to the interests of the proletariat, since a reverse invasion of the
bourgeoisie cannot be excluded in any war and, accordingly, resistance to such
an invasion must be organized.
[Establishing a new relation with new components in this new relation in place of
the relation {Entente (Great Britain, France, Russia) / Triple Alliance (Germany,
Austria-Hungary, Italy)} (since new forces that entered into a new confrontation
and war were awakened: {proletarian plus national revolution / bourgeois plus
national-chauvinistic war}) requires finding a new correlation of these new
components in the indicated relation, i.e. finding a new balance of the new forces
that have entered the arena.
Thus, analyzing the predatory conflict through the prism of the previously
incorrectly composed relation {people (instigated by the bourgeoisie) of one
country / people (instigated by the bourgeoisie) of another country} and
showing the hypocrisy and duplicity of the slogans of such falsely understood
patriotism, Lenin moves on to a more correctly composed and substantiated
relation from such components {proletariat of the conflicting countries /
bourgeoisie of the conflicting countries} and already, proceeding from this new
relation, one has to look for the correlation of its components, the direction of
variability of this correlation and the direction of change (new forms) of the
relation itself].
Theoretically, Lenin says, it would be completely erroneous to forget that any
war is only a continuation of politics by other means: the current imperialist war
is a continuation of the imperialist policy of two groups of great powers, and this
policy is generated and fed by the totality of relations of the imperialist epoch.
But the same epoch must necessarily generate and feed the policy of struggle
against national oppression, and the struggle of the proletariat against the
bourgeoisie, and therefore — the possibility and inevitability of revolutionary
national uprisings and wars, on the one hand, and wars and uprisings of the
proletariat against the bourgeoisie, on the other hand, as well as the unification
of both types of revolutionary wars [8, p. 754]
To this, Lenin adds the following consideration: one cannot forget that we
live in a class society and there is and can be no other way out of it than
through the class struggle. In any class society (based on slavery, serfdom,
wage labor), the oppressing class is armed (the military and the militia are taken
into account — otherwise, in the absence of means of suppression, social
inequality or imbalance could not be maintained for an indefinitely long

time). The arming of the bourgeoisie against the proletariat is one of the most
basic facts of capitalist society. And to proclaim the slogan of disarmament for
the proletariat against this background is tantamount to abandoning the point of
view of the class struggle. On the contrary, the arming of the proletariat is the
only possible tactic of the revolutionary class, a tactic dictated by the entire
objective development of capitalist militarism, prescribed by this development.
And therefore our slogan should be the arming of the proletariat [i.e. increasing
the contentfulness of the form (the proletarian class) at a certain — force — sublevel of this form] in order to defeat and disarm the bourgeoisie. Saying that
"capitalist society was and always is horror without end", Lenin masterfully notes
the trend of piling up contentful accumulations conditioned by military actions,
leading to "an end with horror" (a terrible end). And at the same time, contrary
to established beliefs, behind this "terrible end" he sees the emergence and
emergence of a new form and, accordingly, a new perspective, consisting in the
fact that "before everyone's eyes, by the forces of the bourgeoisie itself, the only
legitimate and revolutionary war is being prepared, namely: civil war against the
imperialist bourgeoisie" [8, p. 755] with a new contentful turn in the course of
the upcoming contentful transformations and transformations.
"Now militarization penetrates all public life" [8, p. 756]: today the imperialist
bourgeoisie militarizes the entire male population, including youth, and in the
plans for tomorrow — the militarization of women as well. Regarding this, one
can say: so much the better, the sooner and closer to the armed uprising against
capitalism. Mastering military science is necessary for the proletariat not in order
to shoot at their brothers, workers of other countries, as is done in the current
war, but in order to fight against the bourgeoisie of their own country, to put an
end to exploitation, poverty and wars not by kind wishes and hopes, by
statements of condemnation and concern, but by victory over the bourgeoisie
and disarming it, which is achieved only by waging war against war, i.e. by civil
confrontation and civil war.
The main disadvantage of the demand for disarmament lies precisely in the fact
that all concrete questions of the revolution are left unattended (ignored). The
"disarmament" of the proletariat (people), Lenin insists, professed by conciliators
with the bourgeoisie of all stripes (opportunists, following Lenin's terminology), is
precisely an escape from the vile reality, and not a struggle against it [the
fundamental transformation of the established reality is possible only by decisive
actions with weapons in hand]. The struggle against imperialism, not connected
with the struggle against opportunism, is an empty phrase and a deception. The
main danger emanating from all varieties of opportunism [i.e. conciliation and
apostasy] is that they hush up (or talk out) the question of the connection of the
ongoing war with the revolution and thus come out with a new absurd slogan:
revolution without weapons [8, pp. 757-758].
Furthermore, the bourgeoisie of all great imperialist powers has become so
reactionary [i.e. striving to avoid crisis and restore the lost balance in affairs]
and so imbued with the striving for world domination [i.e. to achieve
their contentful superiority] that any war on the part of the bourgeoisie of these

countries can only be reactionary. The proletariat should be not only against any
such war, but should wish for the defeat of "its" government in such wars and
use it for a revolutionary armed uprising, if an uprising with the aim of
preventing the war fails [8, pp. 758-759].
With the conciliators (fighters for the disarmament of the people) one cannot
fight by means of programs alone, but only by means of relentless observation
to ensure that they are actually implemented. The largest fatal mistake of the
bankrupt Second International was that the word did not correspond to the
deed, that a habit of hypocrisy and shameless revolutionary phrase was
cultivated. Disarmament as a social idea is generated, obviously, by the special
"peaceful" living conditions of individual small states, which have stood aside

The petty-bourgeois desire to stay away from the great battles of world history,
to use their relatively monopolistic position for passivity — this is the objective
social environment that can provide the idea of disarmament with a certain

reactionary and rests on illusions, for imperialism one way or another drags
small states into the whirlpool of the world economy and world politics. [This
"wonderful" side or feature of the arrangement of "well-fed" lazy, arrogantly
haughty communities and societies must always be taken into account: their
impudently-pushy essence can be overcome by active persistent impact on them

they are not able to withstand a long siege and continuous onslaught, leading to
their physiological discomfort or threatening complete physical destruction].
Switzerland, for example, its imperialist situation prescribes two lines of the

[Both lines are conditioned by the special position and the historically established

Proceeding from these two pronounced tendencies, it becomes clear, Lenin sums

7. In the article "Three Crises" [9, pp. 124-127], written by Lenin in July 1917,
various forms of the course of the revolution are considered (primarily from class
positions). Three objective political crises that determined the nature of the
course of the revolutionary process of that period served as the basis for lies and
slander [i.e. numerous "fakes", as they would say in our time] about the artificial
organization of these crises by the hands of the Bolsheviks. Objectively common
to all three crises is the "overflowing" discontent of the people with the
bourgeoisie and its government, which allows us to see behind all this the
incessant class struggle. Individually in these crises — these are different forms
of conducting (manifestation) of the mentioned class struggle. In all three cases
— this is a demonstration, but: in the first case — stormy-spontaneous,
unorganized, leading to shooting at the demonstrators by the Black Hundreds; in

the second case — appointed by the Bolsheviks, canceled and postponed to a
new date, a general more powerful demonstration with the predominance of
Bolshevik slogans; in the third case — a spontaneous rise of counter-revolution
contrary to the efforts of the Bolsheviks.
An instructive conclusion from the events under consideration, as Lenin notes, is
"that all three crises show us a certain. new in the history of our revolution, form
of a demonstration of a more complex type, with a wave-like movement [from
extreme to extreme], a rapid rise and a steep fall, with an aggravation of the
revolution and counter-revolution, with the "washing out", for a more or less
long time, of the middle elements" [9, pp. 125-126].
Lenin masterfully notes that in such periods of acute political crises, the
contradictions between the main "players" of the revolutionary action are
aggravated [in this case between the components of the very main relation
{proletariat/bourgeoisie}], while some intermediate "middle elements"
accompanying every turmoil are pushed aside: "But the point is that this is not
an ordinary demonstration, it is much more than a demonstration and less than
a revolution. This is an explosion of revolution and counter-revolution together,
this is a sharp, sometimes almost sudden "washing out" of the middle elements
in connection with the stormy manifestation of proletarian and bourgeois
[elements]" [9, p. 126].
The objective clash of two opposing sides, two classes — the proletariat and the
bourgeoisie — corresponds to the political struggle of their parties — the
Bolsheviks and the Cadets. At the same time, the "middle elements" accuse the
Cadets that they facilitate the work of the Bolsheviks, and the Bolsheviks that
they facilitate the work of the Cadets. Now, Lenin says, if instead of political
names we substitute class ones, then we get the dreams of the petty bourgeoisie
about the "disappearance" of the class struggle between the proletariat and the
bourgeoisie. And then follows an objective and dialectically verified conclusion
that no Bolsheviks in the world could have "caused" not only three, but even one
"popular movement", if the deepest economic and political reasons did not
set the proletariat in motion, that no Cadets and monarchists together can
cause any movement "from the right", if equally deep reasons did not create
the counter-revolutionary nature of the bourgeoisie as a class [9, pp.
126-127].
This is an indicator of the petty bourgeoisie's misunderstanding of the
mechanism or essence of the class struggle of the proletariat against the
bourgeoisie. Since the vast majority of the country's population is pettybourgeois in their life situation and even more so in their ideas, and in the
confrontation with large capital (in the face of banks and syndicates in the first
place) the urban proletariat is not yet capable of immediately winning over the
majority of the semi-proletarians and other strata of the population, such an
objectively formed situation [corresponding to the objectively formed at the
current moment correlation of force-components in the main class relation

{proletariat/bourgeoisie}] will be expressed in the inevitability of crises similar to
the three crises under consideration in their three forms.
[Economic and political crises (the confrontation of two deeply non-equilibrium
components of the known relation {proletariat/bourgeoisie}) set in motion both
the revolutionary proletariat (with its form of government or power — the
Soviets of workers', soldiers' and peasants' deputies) on the one hand, and the

inevitable, which leads to both a change in the established correlation between

That is why, in the final analysis, for the implementation of a full-fledged

from a victorious beginning... to a victorious end. This is exactly what
is required of the Russian Soviet Socialist Republic in order to cease
to be poor and powerless, in order to irrevocably become mighty and
abundant" [9, pp. 572-573].

a new relation of components with a perspective of increasing
the contentful component (due to the growth of the economy) and an

the horizon].

5. "Theses of the Central Committee of the RCP(b) in connection with
the situation on the Eastern Front" [10, pp. 145-147] of April 11, 1919
played a huge role in the mobilization of the proletarian masses in the
struggle against Kolchak.
Kolchak's victories on the Eastern Front force both party organizations and
trade unions to implement the following measures in the shortest possible
time to involve broader layers of the working class in the active participation
in the defense of the country.
Comprehensive support for the announced mobilization. The
mobilized must see the active participation of trade unions and feel the
support of their working class. From each party and trade union organization,
the Central Committee demands a weekly, albeit the shortest, report on what
has been done to help the mobilization and the mobilized.
In the frontline areas, it is necessary to carry out the universal
arming of all members of trade unions. "Trade unions must everywhere with
their own forces and means carry out a verification registration of their
members for sending all those not absolutely necessary at home, to fight for
the Volga and the Urals" [10, p. 146].
The most serious attention should be paid to strengthening agitation
among the mobilized, mobilized and Red Army men: not to limit oneself to
lectures and rallies, but to ensure that every member of the trade union
personally participates in agitation events and in personal conversations.
"Replace all male employees with women. Conduct a new reregistration for this, both party and trade union. Introduce special cards for
all members of trade unions and all employees with a note on personal
participation in the matter of helping the Red Army" [10, p. 146].

with published addresses and notification of them to the population in the

6. The article "An Example of the Petrograd Workers" [10, pp. 213-215]
appeared in October 1919 in parallel with the resolution of the plenum of the
Central Committee of the RCP(b) to carry out the mobilization of communists
and send the best representatives of the party and the working class to the
Southern Front in connection with the difficult situation that had developed
there.
The workers of Petrograd, at the call of the party, began an intensified
mobilization and sending of the best workers to fight against Denikin, who was
actively helped with money and weapons by French and English capital. At the
same time, foreign propaganda was working to intimidate the population and the
personnel of the revolutionary troops.

Never before has the enemy been so close to Moscow, Lenin writes. "But to repel
this danger, in addition to the former forces of the troops, we are moving new
detachments of advanced workers capable of creating a turning point in the
mood in the retreating units" [10, p. 214].
The disorganization of the army ranks was largely caused by a large number of
deserters. In this regard, the support of the army by a new influx of proletarian
forces is of paramount importance. "Unstable elements will be strengthened, the
mood will be raised, a turning point will be achieved. The proletariat, as has
constantly happened in our revolution, will support and direct the wavering
strata of the working population" [10, p. 214].
For a long time, the Petrograd proletariat has borne more burdens than the
workers of other industrial centers; on its shoulders are the struggle against
hunger, and against military danger, and the pulling out of the best workers to
Soviet positions throughout Russia. But there is not the slightest despondency or
decline of strength among the Petrograd workers — they perfectly fulfill the task
of the vanguard detachment, sending help and support where it is most needed.
"When such fresh forces go to strengthen the faltering parts of our army, then
the working masses, soldiers from the peasants, receive new leaders from
among their own people, from among the more developed, more conscious,
more spiritually firm workers" [10, p. 214]. This is the source of the superiority
of the peasant army over the enemy, which replenishes and supports its ranks
only with "landlords' sons". It is known that such "support" ruined Kolchak, it will
ruin Denikin too. Lenin calls on other workers, following the example of the
Petrograd workers, to intensify their activities in the troops, to show more
initiative and boldness in the desire not to yield to the Petrogradites — and then
victory will be for the workers, the bourgeois counter-revolution will be finished
off. After Petrograd, Moscow has already started moving, after Moscow the rest
must start moving.
[From the content of the article it is clearly visible how important the moral state
(state of spirit) of the fighting soldiers of the Red Army is for Lenin. It is visible
how truly significant the high consciousness of the advanced workers and
members of the Bolshevik party was in those days! Again, it is visible how strong
and unshakable Lenin's faith in victory and a favorable outcome of the armed
struggle is, — his inexhaustible optimism in the most difficult moments of a
mortal struggle causes deep respect.]
7. The article "From the Destruction of the Centuries-old Order to the
Creation of a New One" [10, pp. 282-284], written in April 1920, is devoted to
the issues set out in the one-day newspaper "Communist Subbotnik" of April 11,
1920. The main topic of the newspaper is the question of communist labor. This
most important issue of the construction of socialism could be raised only after
the proletariat had conquered political power, only after the expropriation of
landlords and capitalists, only after the decisive victories of the proletariat that
had conquered state power over the exploiters who had organized desperate
resistance, counter-revolutionary uprisings and civil war. It seemed that such a

time had already come in early 1918, but a new, stronger wave of counterrevolutionary uprisings broke out so quickly that the Soviet power then had no
opportunity to deal with the issues of peaceful construction. Now, after
overcoming incredible difficulties and "unheard-of victories of the Red Army over
the hordes of international capitalist reaction" there are chances that peace will
be more lasting and stable.
Some experience in the construction of socialism already exists, therefore the
question of communist labor can and must be raised point-blank. However, it
would be more correct to say: not about communist, but about socialist labor,
for we are talking not about the highest, but about the lowest stage of the
development of the new social order growing out of capitalism. Communist labor
is unpaid, voluntary labor, outside the norm, out of the habit of working for the
common good and out of a conscious (turned into a habit) attitude to the
necessity of labor as a need of a healthy organism. However, "it is clear to
everyone that it is still very, very far to the wide, truly mass application of such
labor for us, i.e. for our society, our social order" [10, p. 283].
But the fact that this question has already been raised by the advanced
proletariat and the state power is a step forward. To come to the big, one must
start with the small. "Subbotniks, labor armies, labor conscription — this is the
practical implementation in various forms of socialist and communist labor" [10,
p. 283].
Mistakes and errors in such a difficult matter are inevitable. "To build a new labor
discipline, to build new forms of social connection between people, to build new
forms and techniques of attracting people to labor, this is the work of many
years and decades. This is the most grateful and noble work" [10, p. 283].
Now, after the overthrow of the bourgeoisie and the suppression of its
resistance, there is already soil on which such work has become possible; the
proletariat will take up this work with all its energy and will fulfill it.
[Again, the inexhaustible optimism of V.I. Lenin is admirable. The thesis about
labor out of habit as a need of a healthy organism is closely connected with the
thesis about the contentful filling of the forms of the labor process to the level of
the identification of these forms in the final analysis with the very process of
cognition itself and with the striving through such forms of the cognitive
process to look around the corner, beyond the horizon, into the future as
effectively and productively as possible.]
Conclusion
It should be said that the above-mentioned articles of Lenin were not selected
specially or with any particular bias — any of his works of the indicated periods is
built according to the stencil we have disassembled, according to the type of
thinking that was formed in V.I. Ulyanov from a young age.
As can be seen, every article or work of Lenin, regardless of the range of issues
considered in it, is a model of the effective application of the dialectical approach,

extremely in demand and necessary when conducting research of objects,
processes, phenomena in any area of reality, in all spheres of human activity in
order to obtain the most correct and contentful results of such research.
In each work, the scheme of productive ("triadic" or three-stage) development of
the idea (thesis — antithesis — synthesis) is easily guessed, as well as
adherence (in the analysis) to the well-known cliché of observing the unity of the
historical and the logical.
What first of all attracts attention in the course of getting acquainted with
the content of the theoretical works of V.I. Lenin? The same or similar algorithm
for presenting material concerning the issues under study, which is due to the
very universal nature of development or the very dialectical nature of any object,
process, phenomenon under study. And Lenin inexorably follows this algorithm,
revealing the dialectical essence of the problems under study ("piling up" events,
emerging situations, achieved results, etc.).
Let us reproduce this algorithm once again.
1. Composing correct relations of directly interacting side-components in the
forms (objects, processes, phenomena) under study.
At this initial stage of research, one must be able to identify and show the
directly interacting (i.e. the most essential at the moment) opposing, competing,
opposing sides-components in the forms-relations under study.
2. Determination and prioritization in the found relations, i.e. identification of the
active and passive sides-components in the composed relations.
At this stage of research among the directly interacting components in their
relations (as a rule, in pairs of such components) one must again be able to
identify, guess, understand and highlight, on the one hand, the
most contentful (active, leading) and, on the other hand, more formal (passive,
conservative) components. Thus, such relations are brought to the generalized
universal form {content/form}.
3. Determination of the probable direction of change (transformation),
movement, development of the constituent sides-components (i.e. probable
change in the correlation or balance of components) within and inside the
relations formed from them, and consequently, the direction of variability of the
relations themselves as a whole (taking into account the influences and impact
of surrounding forms on these relations).
A correctly composed relation of two correctly identified directly interacting
components allows to determine and designate the bias or direction of their
subsequent change, movement, development of their relation; this bias or
direction of movement-development of the relation is conditioned by the
correlation (approximate) of components within the relation, i.e. the degree of
predominance of one (contentful) component over the other (formal)
component.

4. Forecasting (anticipation) of the development of the relations under study, i.e.
determination of the prospects for the modification of the forms under study
(objects, processes, phenomena), as well as the prospects for the emergence of
new forms.
At this stage, both operational and strategic forecasts of the development of the
forms-relations under study are made, which corresponds to forecasting the
variability of existing forms and anticipating the emergence of new forms of
reality.
When moving to the next stage of research or the next form of reality under
study, paragraphs 1-4 are repeated in the same volume.
As can be seen from the presented scheme (algorithm) of conducting research,
the identification of two (or more) directly interacting, opposing components in
their relation turns out to be an extremely important point, since it is
this Content-form structure (system) that will indicate the direction of its change
and obtaining in the future an even more contentful structure or a new relation
(system) composed of even more contentful components. It is important that
these are not "pulled by the ears" components, not mechanically (or eclectically)
combined opposites, but naturally growing one from the other opposing
constituent sides, interconnected so much that one can firmly talk about their
insignificance without each other.
A correctly composed relation of two directly interacting components is
something (a system) resembling the behavior of a liquid (or other similar
continuous medium) in communicating vessels. Here, for example, are such
relations
(or
systems)
as:
{time/space},
{motion/rest},
{active
principle/passive principle}, {energy/mass}, {field/matter}, {north pole of a
magnet/south pole of a magnet}, {subjective /objective}, etc., etc., — they are
examples of correctly composed relations of directly interacting sidescomponents of reality.
The correct choice of a pair of interacting components and the formation of a
correct relation from them is tantamount to an artfully formulated question; then
the very correct formulation of the question contains at least half of the answer
to this question, which is a prerequisite for the successful solution of the task at
hand.
Well, and examples of incorrectly composed relations can be given much more —
all unfairly conducted research and obtained false results sufficiently testify to
the initially incorrect relations identified [inappropriately chosen and designated
so-called false grounds lead any research to a dead end]. Therefore, it is
extremely important to identify the most essential sides of the problems under
study, which are in direct interaction and direct relation to each other.
At each stage of the development of reality, at each specific spatio-temporal
segment of it, one must be able to correctly identify and identify (i.e. recognize)
the directly interacting components that are in contradiction or conflict with each

other, conditioned (we are talking about contradiction or conflict) by the search
for a new form for the developing contentful component.
From the listed steps of the above scheme (algorithm), a methodology (and
even more — a whole methodology) for the effective research of any issue, any
problem, both in everyday life and in the field of science (so to speak, a
universal research template) is formed.
Of course, in that former form Lenin's works (and they were written on topical
issues in connection with some specific events and circumstances) today to some
extent lose their formal significance, but "pushing" the author into the
background for this reason is as reckless and foolish as, for example, giggling at
the fact that Alexander the Great fought all his life on horseback, and not in a
tank or an airplane. After all, in terms of contentful filling and presentation of
views, his works are, without any exaggeration, masterpieces (for those
obsessed with the idea of knowing the essence of dialectics). One just needs to
look more pedantically into the composition of any of his articles to see the
beauty of the logical-dialectical schemes, constructions and forms. The future
requires a person to be more and more versed in the issues of dialectics.
As can be seen, in our research and conclusions we do not particularly touch
upon the temporal context, focusing mainly on Lenin's logic — all the pearls of
the research approach shine without a deep attachment to the nuances of that
great historical era. The historical background in each particular case gives the
development process only peculiar shades and features, leaving its core (i.e.
tendencies and patterns) typical and universal. In this perspective, any article by
Lenin, any of his works is a reader on dialectics, a manual on mastering the art
of dialectical thinking (to some extent invariant with respect to specific historical
frameworks), i.e. on mastering what belongs to the future. No one has been able
to understand and feel the "soul" of dialectics so deeply for a long time, to the
greatest regret.
And how much optimism and faith (namely due to the dialectical nature of the
character) in a favorable, positive outcome of the matter is laid in each of his
articles! After all, at times the entire revolutionary movement hung on such a
thin hair that it was time to fall into despair and run anywhere, fearing the
reaction. He did not flinch once, and there are no traces of retreat or repentance
found to this day by either "impartial" historians or rabid enemies. Only forward
— to the victorious heights of scientific thinking.
It would seem that Russia has a full set of conditions and resources (both human
and natural) to be the most self-sufficient and harmoniously developing state on
the planet. And the understanding of the direction, trend, perspective of the
movement-development of the people and the country (where and why are we
going?) has been lost since the times of the last most integral dialectician, which
was V.I. Lenin. The idea (in the most general form — ideology, and not just
ideology, but the most advanced ideology, which Lenin was guided by) as the
most perfect derivative of human consciousness and is the guiding star, which is

the most content-capacious of all reliably known to us forms of our (human)
level of development of reality, — why the most contentful flows of the level of
human consciousness strive to possess this form (to clothe themselves in this
form). So what, it would seem, is simpler — take and learn dialectics from Lenin
(master, so to speak, the dialectical style of thinking and advanced ideas, which
are an inevitable product of this kind of thinking) instead of subjecting him to
ostracism. But no: to grow up to the perception of his ideas, it turns out, is much
more difficult than to revile, showing off one's not so perfect brains and very
limited abilities.
Marxism is supposedly too orthodox, and Lenin's ideas have led to a dead
end and have lost all relevance — these are the arguments of today's
academic community (primarily among economists, politicians, historians,
political scientists, etc.). Enlightened people, however, say that one should
learn to follow not the letter of Marxism, not the letter of Lenin's
manuscripts, but their spirit. The spirit (contentful principle) of dialectics is
eternal: it only passes from form to form, i.e. it simply changes the form of
its acquiring. And to learn to grasp, to perceive this spirit — this is the
purpose and main goal of the life and activity of every educated person.
The approach described here is intended to direct a person not just to the
knowledge of some world that has existed since time immemorial, but
predisposes to a meaningful study of everything that "is being done", that "is
being reproduced" and "is being recreated" right here and now — in real-time
mode both in our near and far environment.
Another appeal to Lenin's words that even 70 Marxs will not be able to cover
the "sum of all these changes in all their branches" and cope with solving
such a task, contributes to the realization of the circumstance that any, albeit
the most adequate at the moment, solution models the behavior of a very
limited, "trimmed", "frozen" system, while reality is constantly changing and
slips out from under any rigid and comprehensive control. That is why one
must learn to perceive and grasp not the volumes of all kinds of changes of
real forms, but the trend of these changes, which is ultimately the key and
condition for the successful cognition of developing reality and the
comprehension of objective truth.
With an eye to what has been said, it becomes obvious how one should
relate to the question of the cognizability of the world. Since the world, the
Universe are incessantly and unceasingly "being made", "being recreated by
piling up" (according to the formulation of the principle of self-development),
and do not represent a kind of suitcase with books, then the cognition of the
being made, every moment reproduced world — this is all the more an
infinite and infinitely fascinating contentful process. How can one talk about
some kind of comprehensive, complete and final knowledge of the world if
the world is incessantly "unfolding" with more and more contentful forms? In
this section, it is more reasonable to talk about the cognizability and
cognition of the world as about the improvement of capabilities for

anticipating such an "unfolding" of forms, capabilities for guessing the
emergence of new ones and the trend of changing existing forms, i.e. for
forecasting future events and their consequences, speaking in the usual
everyday language. Effective forecasting of the development of the forms of
reality — this is the perspective of the contentful development of mankind. In
times when, due to the infinitely routine everyday cyclicity, a person sees no
meaning in life and in his existence, an opportunity opens up to look at all
this with completely different eyes. Every person is not just capable, but is
obliged to become a researcher. In each of us, a subconscious striving is
dormant to manage to look around the corner, beyond the horizon (satisfying
one's innate curiosity), to anticipate the onset of events, to forecast and
anticipate the emergence and development of new forms. It is only
necessary to activate this dormant natural striving and give it a new impulse,
i.e. consciously bring it to a new level.
There is not a single argument refuting or at least questioning the validity
(universality and generality) of the dialectical concept of the development of
reality — undoubtedly, it must be mastered and learn to correctly and
effectively apply it.
