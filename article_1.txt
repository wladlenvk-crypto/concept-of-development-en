ARTICLE 1
Concept of Development: The Foundational Principle
Abstract
This article is devoted to the exposition of a worldview concept based on the
awareness of the significance and role of the relations of the widely known
foundational aspects of reality: content and form.
Content and form are two inalienable constituent sides of any element of
reality, be it an object, a process, or a phenomenon. By virtue of the
omnipresence of content and form, the relations of these components —
these two principles of reality — prove to be the most important and
essential.
In the course of analyzing the aforementioned relations, the internal source
of evolutionary movement and the "mechanism" of its functioning are
revealed, on the basis of which the directionality of evolutionary processes is
defined and formulated for the first time (i.e., an idea of the vector of
change, movement, and development of the surrounding world and its
elements is given).

The content-form transformations of unitary directionality described in the
article, which underlie all real processes, not only explain the spiral-cyclical
nature of the world but also make it possible to forecast the prospect of its
development. It is precisely this mode of interaction of the fundamental
aspects (principles) of reality that ensures the irreversibility of the evolution
of forms, as well as the novelty, uniqueness, and inexhaustible diversity of
the latter.
The approach under consideration fundamentally levels any "centrism"
("absolutism") of whatever form, pointing both to the limited applicability of
the currently dominant anthropocentric (as well as bio-, theo-, and
sociocentric) model of the world, and to the necessity of transition to
another, more integral and adequate model of it.
This concept may be recommended to the attention of researchers primarily
in view of its integrity (in contrast to the fragmentary pictures of the world
and theories of the past and present), as well as by virtue of the following
considerations: whoever possesses the most adequate worldview — the
future belongs to them.
Introduction
A reader inclined toward the philosophical comprehension of natural-scientific
and humanitarian knowledge has surely had occasion to reflect on the fact
that at the basis of real processes lie, and govern the surrounding world,
deep-lying regularities different from those that are discovered on the
surface and perceived as self-evident. We are speaking of objective
fundamental relations that condition, among other things, the functioning
and structure of the economy, politics, science, culture, and other aspects of
the life of human communities.
On the basis of many years of tracking discoveries and achievements in
various fields of the natural and humanitarian sciences, I will venture to state
that to this day what takes place everywhere is chiefly the mere
accumulation and mechanical "warehousing" of factual material, and there is
as yet not a single contentful unifying idea that would allow us finally to
realize what the observed and "warehoused" phenomena actually represent.
In other words, the need for an integral and adequate model of everything
that exists, develops, and evolves is felt ever more acutely.
I believe hardly anyone would object to the proposition that knowledge and
understanding of the aforementioned regularities would contribute to raising
the accuracy of analysis and forecasting (based on this analysis) of the
course of real events.
The article presents a non-standard view of the principles of organization of
living and non-living nature, of the origins and directionality of evolution;
after acquainting himself with it, a reader interested in such questions may

develop a motivation for a critical rethinking of established worldview
attitudes and a revision of the methods of analyzing the factual material and
information obtained, which will ultimately be expressed in an increase in the
effectiveness of the cognitive process.
This is, perhaps, the most integral and adequate concept available today,
encompassing the spheres of interest of numerous scientific collectives and
making it possible to comprehend the principles of correct forecasting of the
development of reality.
Below it will be shown WHAT in fact lies at the basis of the development of
reality, which is accomplished in many respects through inversion (i.e., the
"turning over" of roles, methods, and notions, in Marx's figurative
expressions, or "return into the foundation," in Hegel's terminology [12, p.
102]).
Thus, the reader's attention is offered a "thesis" version of the concept [1;
20], which examines the system of natural-scientific views and
representations concerning the evolutionary movement and development of
reality from the standpoint of implicit but objective deep-lying regularities
that underlie these views and representations.
The essence of the proposed concept of development and its
foundational principle
Traditionally, one speaks of the movement and development of anything,
first of all, as of change: nothing remains for any length of time equal and
identical to itself. At the same time, it is impossible to speak of changes
apart from connection with what already exists. Stars, planets, oceans, and
mountains change; plants, animals, people, and the products of human
activity change even faster and more strikingly. In other words, changes are
never isolated and abstract, but are always attached to what exists — to
objects, processes, phenomena, i.e., to some already existing basis. At that,
it is not possible to separate the changing from the existing, i.e., change
from basis, which compels us to speak of the movement and development of
any object, process, or phenomenon as of a derivative of a certain symbiosis
— of basis and change.
In general form, it is convenient to speak of such a symbiosis in terms of the
bilateral, two-component relation {content/form}, i.e., in the notions of
the categorical apparatus developed sufficiently deeply before us by our
outstanding predecessors-dialecticians. Of the movement and development
of any object, process, or phenomenon one can speak as of a contentful
change of its form. The contentful changes that take place in reality manifest
themselves through changing forms. Indeed, if one scrupulously analyzes the
changes and transformations occurring in our surroundings (equally as in
ourselves), one can see that behind all changes and transformations there is
implicitly discovered a single-in-essence process of the movement of a

certain contentful activity through a series, a chain, a sequence of changing
forms.
In order to avoid misunderstanding and divergent readings, let us cite the
textbook interpretation of the notions "content" and "form" [13, pp. 621,
622]. Content is the determining side of the whole, the aggregate and unity
of all its constituent parts, properties, internal processes, contradictions, and
tendencies; form is the mode of existence and expression of content. In the
interrelation of content and form, content represents the dynamic side of the
whole, while form encompasses the system of its stable connections.
The relation of content and form is characterized by a unity reaching the
point of their transition into each other; however, this unity is relative.
Content is not the substrate by itself, but its internal state, the aggregate of
processes, and in this sense content itself acts as a process. The
understanding of form, in turn, presupposes the consideration of it as a
developing and becoming structure.
Content, being the active side, represents the mobile (as a rule, internal)
constituent of the relation {content/form} and symbolizes change (at times
quite hidden, veiled). Form, at that, as the passive external constituent-side
of the same relation, follows the active internal content.
As the active constituent side of the relation, content is internally inherent in
a certain impulse, stimulus, urge, motivation toward change, movement,
development. This is, first of all, what distinguishes the active contentful side
from the formal passive one. Then change, movement, and development
themselves do not arise suddenly and do not appear as some separate,
isolated, and independent element, but prove to be a derivative or
consequence of the interaction of unequal, dissimilar constituent sides in
their relation and striving to equalize, to identify with, and to become each
other.
Meanwhile, at every attempt to single out either of these two constituent
sides in "pure" form, without the "admixture" of its other, we get into a fix —
both sides, upon closer examination, sink their roots one into the other, bear
simultaneously the features of their other, which testifies, apparently, to
their deep syncretism.
Extremely important in this connection is Hegel's point of view on the
interrelation of content and form, who believed that "content is not formless,
while form is at one and the same time both contained in content itself and
represents something external to it," which reduces to the absolute relation
"between form and content, namely — their transition into each other, so
that content is nothing other than the transition of form into content, and
form — the transition of content into form" [2, p. 298].
In this way the constituents in the relation {content/form} interact with each
other, or more precisely, as it were, "live" by the striving to become each

other and to absorb each other completely and without residue, by the
striving to turn into identity with each other, by the striving toward mutual
transition, mutual interpenetration, mutual transformation, mutual
expression...
Since it is simply impossible either to single out or to separate content from
form (with respect to concrete objects, processes, phenomena), the most
productive, rational, and correct way is to speak only of the relation of
these two mutually interpenetrating constituents-components-sides of
concrete objects, processes, and phenomena.
In this light, every concrete object, process, or phenomenon is the relation of
its sides or constituents {content/form}, and, in the final analysis, what
matters here is not each constituent side by itself, but precisely their
relation; the development of any object, process, or phenomenon, in turn,
is the expression or representation of changing content by means of
changing forms and, ultimately, it is the process of becoming and
manifestation of each such concrete form in the form of a definite
correlation (relative balance) of the aforementioned constituent sides
(with the possibility of expressing and representing the said correlation,
including in mathematical or digital form).
Against the background and in the terms of these representations, the
following simple idea acquires a more or less coherent shape: everything that
we see, hear, and feel, everything with which and about which we think, i.e.,
everything that surrounds us, including ourselves, — these are various
formations or forms of different levels (different degrees) of contentfulness,
into which a certain contentful activity is clothed in its movement from
level to level.
Such formations-forms represent dipoles (spirals) of two poles (two
branches), one of which is the contentful active constituent side of the dipole
(spiral), the second — its (its) passive formal constituent side; in the
philosophical aspect, the aforementioned dipole (spiral) is similar to the
relation of two abstract notions {content/form}. An approximate visual
model of such a dipole-spiral is given in Fig. 1.

Continuing to proceed from real relations and basing ourselves on them, we
follow further in our reasoning. Interacting with each other, dipole-forms of
different levels generate dipole-forms of new levels. The contentful
constituent, being the active side, strives to escape the control of any and
every form that encompasses and contains it; however, not having the
possibility to realize itself otherwise than again through some form, it is
forced each time to clothe itself in the next, unrelentingly following changing
form. In prospect and in the ultimate expression, this process takes the form
of an endless "chase" of the contentful constituent (content) after the very
same contentful constituent moving and developing in such an uncomplicated
manner. [One can arrive at such a conclusion by using, for example, the
device of the limiting transition, which mathematicians apply when finding
the limits of functions.]
Modifying and adapting the initial model (Fig. 1) in line with what has just
been said, we arrive at the following conceptual form of it, which brings us
close to understanding the directionality of the course and development of
all real processes, i.e., the vector of development of anything whatsoever.
In a more or less laconic form, the formulation of the directionality of the
development of reality looks as follows: the development of content is
accomplished in the direction of the exhaustive expression
(encompassment, absorption, assimilation, appropriation, comprehension...)
by the developing content of the very content developing in such a
way.
Let us denote the formulation by the symbol @ for the convenience of further
handling of it.
With respect to the surrounding world, this formulation means approximately
the following: everything that exists and develops has as the direction of its
development and existence the expression (encompassment, absorption,
assimilation, appropriation, comprehension...) by itself, the existing and
developing, of itself, the existing and developing.
Taking into account the previously noted inseparability of the components of
content and form in their actual relation {content/form}, it becomes obvious
that the external manifestation of the above-described process of contentful
accumulation @ proves to be the renewal of the form of expression of the
accumulating content by raising the "storey-ness" or level of this very form
of its (of the accumulated content) representation. As indeed happens in
reality, the movement of content determined by formulation @ cannot be
realized otherwise than through forms of different levels of representation of
this content that do not repeat in details.

Thus, we are speaking of the description of an infinite, unceasing process of
contentful movement, realized by means of successive approximations to the
exhaustive expression of the very contentful movement; in this way, in
conceptual-abstract language, a multilevel formation (form) is described,
whose constituent parts (branches), like the formation as a whole, are similar
to "self-winding" spirals.
Within the conceptual (notional) formulation @, which models the
development of multilevel reality, in a "sublated" (as philosophers say) form
are "packed" both the activity (movement-development), and the
directionality of this activity (movement-development), and the contradiction
(as the source of all movement-development), and the laws of dialectics,
etc., etc.
In essence, a general, single, universal internal principle of selfdevelopment (self-organization) of reality has been formulated, an implicit
or explicit hint (and in other cases — a firm conviction) of the existence of
which is present in the representations and statements of a huge number of
scientists, researchers, and philosophers of the past and present who have
devoted themselves to the study and interpretation of the surrounding world
and its evolutionary movement [3-7; 9-11; 14; 16; 17].
That is why all real processes have a spiral-cyclical character !!!
In other words, by virtue of relations of this kind, our world and all its
elements are spiral-shaped: man is a spiral, a dog is a spiral, an ant is a
spiral, a living cell is a spiral, a molecule is a spiral, an atom is a spiral, an
electron is a spiral, a photon is a spiral, a galaxy is a spiral, the Universe is a
spiral; anyone who wishes may exercise in continuing this list as far as they
like. The brain of two hemispheres, the heart, the lungs, and other internal
organs and systems of man (as, incidentally, of other individuals of the
animal world) likewise represent bipolar spiral structures (see Fig. 1).
On the other hand, it is precisely this mode of interaction of the foundational
components of the said relation {content/form} that ensures the novelty,
uniqueness, and irreversibility of the development and evolution of forms, as
well as the inexhaustible diversity of the latter.
As already noted above, in this way the components-constituents in the
relation {content/form} interact with each other, submitting to the "dictate"
of the objective tendency with directionality toward becoming each other and
absorbing each other completely and without residue.
However, if one gives preference only to this point of view on development
as the movement of the foundational principles-components of reality only in
the direction of convergence and reunification (i.e., thereby dooming
everything visibly existing to a supposedly obvious end and degeneration into
identity), — then how can one explain the presence and the tireless,
unceasing replenishment and renewal of opposites ("dissimilarities") and

their inexhaustible interaction and opposition in the surrounding world? In
what way does the inexhaustible difference then arise and manifest itself
under the conditions of the total striving of everything toward unification and
coincidence proclaimed here?
However paradoxical this may seem, it is precisely by virtue of such a
naturally conditioned character of movement (striving) that renewed
extremes, opposites, and their opposition can only arise and manifest
themselves. The interaction of two initial extremes, of two non-coinciding
principles, of two "sworn" competitors, of two unequal and non-equivalent
forms generates something third, a certain new form, which, along with the
content and attributes "absorbed" into itself of the interacting initial forms,
simultaneously bears in itself also the difference from each of them. [For this
reason, in particular, the "holistic" assertion that the whole is always greater
than the sum of its parts is not without foundation.]
It is precisely the striving of two unequal, non-identical competing
constituents of one relation to become each other in the course of their
interaction (see Fig. 1) that leads to the emergence of something third,
coinciding neither with the first nor with the second. In other words, the
movement of content through the encompassment and absorption into itself
of the content of previous forms constantly generates dissimilarity to
everything preceding, i.e., novelty and uniqueness. But this very
difference arising "in continuation," simultaneously or "in parallel," acquires,
in essence, also the form of a new, next opposite, which immediately begins
to oppose everything existing (and preceding) and, again, to enter into
interaction with it.
Thus, under the declared striving toward unity and coincidence, difference
and dissimilarity (novelty) arise each time as if automatically and derivatively
with respect to the more primordial, more initial, more fundamental and
priority movement toward identity. On the other hand, this derivatively
obtained non-coincidence, in turn, proves to be the cause and ground for the
excitation of the next "act" of movement (striving) in the same direction
toward unification, which again, in its turn, generates a new portion of
difference and novelty, serving as the condition for the continuation of the
"conflict," competition, interaction, etc., etc. — to infinity in its process of
quantitative and qualitative renewal. The very divergence into extremes and
opposites is a consequence of the preceding and simultaneously a cause and
condition for initiating the subsequent movement in the same initially
determined direction — toward the mutual transition and coincidence of
extremes, toward their "drawing together into a point." [Let us note that the
latter is not asserted (and not imposed) as a final result and achieved goal,
but is considered only as an understood and comprehended objective
tendency that subordinates to itself the course of all processes without
exception — as an asymptotic relative movement toward a certain absolute,
but never attained limit.]

It is superfluous to mention that representations of spiral development are
as old as the world and there is no novelty in these representations. What
is new and curious — is in the description of the "mechanism" that produces
spiral-cyclical movement, i.e., in the description of the functioning of the
internal source of such movement.
Heraclitus's saying is widely known that everything flows, everything
changes. But WHERE all this flows and changes — until now there have been
no clear representations. Only today does the possibility open up to answer
this question more or less intelligibly. Today we can meaningfully determine
ourselves with respect to the vector of this universal movement: everywhere
and always there takes place a total multilevel movement in the direction of
achieving the highest level of contentfulness (see formulation @).
Everything without exception is permeated by the striving and embraced by
the movement in the direction of possessing the highest contentfulness. By
this vector of universal movement are determined the causality, meanings,
and goals: of life, development, evolution, history, etc. — i.e., of the being
and existence of anything whatsoever, in whatever forms.
The formulation of the principle of self-development @ gives an idea of the
"unidirectionality" and "multilevelness" of development, i.e., firstly, of the
uniqueness of each of the arising and developing forms-levels (which
fundamentally levels the centrism of any form whatsoever, including
biocentrism, anthropocentrism, theocentrism, sociocentrism, etc.), and,
secondly, of the universality of the mechanism of movement-development of
all forms at their levels (which, at last, throws the long-awaited bridge
between the levels of the non-living, the living, and thinking, extremely in
demand and necessary for the formation of integral and adequate
representations of the world).
By virtue of the inexhaustibility of the interconnection of the contentful and
formal constituent sides in the relation {content/form} noted above,
whatever relative independence the contentful constituent may possess,
nevertheless it too cannot manifest (and keep manifesting) itself otherwise
than through form. Therefore, despite the leadership and seeming autonomy
of the active contentful side at all levels of development, as the contentful
constituent at any of the levels can act, again, only forms (such is the
essence of the "eternal curse" imposed on content — to be burdened with
form, if one paraphrases Marx's well-known expression about the relation of
spirit and matter [8, p. 29]). Thus, in both cases (both in the case of the
active "contentful" constituent and in the case of the passive "formal" side) in
reality one has to deal with changing and developing forms, by which one
should understand all sorts of structures and formations of different
(objective and subjective) levels of reality of varying degrees of
contentfulness (both in macro- and microscopic executions).
Realizing the total unitary directionality of all real processes, exhibited by
formulation @, it is nevertheless necessary to take into account that at each

of its levels of movement the dominant active contentful constituent
(contentfulness) has its own special (specific) forms of representation.
A). At the level of non-living nature — this is, as a rule, the energy of the
Sun and of space (light, heat, radiation, gravitation, high-energy particles),
electrical energy, the chemical energy of oxidation-reduction reactions, etc.
B). At the level of living inanimate nature, in addition to what is named
above in point A — these are nutrients (from the composition of soil, water,
air, combustion products), food (plant and animal), the flow of blood (in
living organisms), the genetic program, reflex-psychic activity, instincts, etc.
C). At the level of human and social nature, in addition to what is named
above at the two previous levels A and B — these are mineral resources, fuel
(coal, oil, gas and their industrial derivatives), the human resource (muscular
force, spiritual and moral forces, creative and intellectual potential), products
of human activity and thinking (image, idea, notions, tool, money, capital,
financial and information flows), etc. In other words, at the level of man and
society the same active contentful constituent circulates in the form of the
forms enumerated in the two previous points A and B, with the addition of
human-mediated forms (including the forms of all sorts of products of human
activity and thinking — technologies and ideologies).

As can be seen on the basis of the analysis of the formulation of the principle
of self-development @, the question of the essence of development is not
exhausted by the understanding of development as the complication of
forms, or as the action of the great biological law of complication, or as the
causal continuous complication of matter, etc. [4; 9; 11] (these explanations
and definitions of the essence of development dominating in the scholarly
milieu are capable today of satisfying at most a housewife); the essence of
any development consists in the achievement by forms of different levels of
development of the highest contentfulness for each level, which is
expressed (and ensured) by the movement of forms in the direction of
possessing the maximally possible contentfulness at their levels.
The formulated principle of self-development @ is universal; all processes
without exception at all levels of reality known to us are subordinated to it
and governed by it: at the level of non-living nature, at the level of living
inanimate nature, and, finally, at the level of man, human thinking, and
society, which can be confirmed and interpreted by diverse factual material,
including simple examples from everyday life [1; 20].
Below, on numerous examples, we will show — how this internal principle of
self-development "works" in different areas and at different levels of the
acquisition of changing reality.
Why, then, are the concepts of such an obvious spiral movementdevelopment-evolution of forms considered obsolete today, are examined
and discussed reluctantly, and are sometimes subjected to harsh criticism on
the part of the official scholarly community? Specialists on questions of
evolution substantiate their disagreement with concepts of this kind by the
fact that in none of the developing forms (objects, processes, phenomena)
does the course of their development resemble (and reduce to) in pure form
an ascending classical spiral, and in the majority of analyzed cases this spiral
is distorted and interrupted by segments of some straight lines unlike spiral
ones (indicating the movement-development of forms by a certain "inertia"),
horizontal plateaus (symbolizing the stagnation of forms) and, finally,
descending segments (meaning regression, depression, decline, etc.).
Indeed, in the study of the evolution of real forms, obvious deviations from
the spiral character of their development are observed and recorded at every
turn, and therefore, probably, the collective spiral as an indicative external
form of expression of any development recedes into the background.

At that, however, the scholarly community loses sight of the following
circumstance. As already noted above, the spiral contentful movement has as
its reverse visible side the formation of new and the filling with content of
renewing forms. In other words, the reverse side of the implicit, hidden
movement of content in the direction @ proves to be the inevitable
emergence and manifestation of modified and new forms of different levels
filled with content, visible and registered by human consciousness, which, as
they arise and are modified, enter into interaction with each other,
continuing their movement-development in accordance with formulation @.
And this objectively conditioned movement toward the increment of
contentfulness of any and every interacting form, which, as already noted
above, is characteristic of all levels-forms without exception, is accompanied
by the total competition of forms (as if we were speaking of participants
in a race to a single finish).
Not having other sources for replenishing and building up their content
except other similar forms, all forms, in the name of ensuring their own
movement and development under the "dictate" of principle @, are forced to
enter into a harsh competitive struggle "for survival" with the forms
surrounding them, the outcome of which proves to be either continuing
development, or the perishing and "tearing apart" into parts (absorption and
assimilation by competitors) of this or that form. Regression, by and large
and on a universal scale, does not happen!
By virtue of the mutual total, universal, and wholesale competition of forms
striving toward one "goal," which is determined again by the principle of selfdevelopment @, forms "withdraw" from each other and "appropriate" for
themselves everything that lends itself to withdrawal and appropriation. The
"uncontrolled" taking away and annexation of portions of content takes place
at different levels and in different chains of development. At that, the objects
of multilevel taking-away-appropriation prove to be quanta of energy,
electrons, ions, genes, chromosomes, cells, portions of substance, objects,
ideas, bits of information, name, glory, reputation, ideologies, technologies,
and entire fortunes (everything, as in real life!). As the experience of
numerous and many-year observations shows, when one form "weakens"
and "dies" — somewhere nearby (inside or outside) another begins to "assert
itself" and "flourish." Stagnation or regression of any form means an
increment of content in some of its neighbors (and perhaps not exactly
neighbors). As soon as at any form at any of its sublevels the initiative is
substantially "intercepted," the "tearing apart" of the content of this form at
all its other sublevels and sub-sublevels immediately begins — up to the
complete liquidation of this form and the assimilation of its content by other
forms. That is why one level-form can quite unexpectedly fade or disappear,
and another begin to develop and modify spontaneously.
Thus, universal and wholesale stagnation or regression does not happen, but
always and everywhere there take place only content-form transformations
(or, speaking in the habitual "school" language, — the transition of the
contentful constituent from one form into another, i.e., from level to level),

during which the decline or loss of content by one form are signs of the
interception of this content and its annexation to itself by another form.
Which of the forms, of what level, at what segment or stage of its
development will hold out in the unceasing competitive struggle and continue
(possibly even more intensively, with a "leap") its further development, and
which will disappear forever — it is extremely difficult to predict; for, by
virtue of universal competition and total rivalry, the process of the
evolution of forms does not have the appearance of a straight high road and
does not fall under the definition of a strictly "tree-like" process (as is noted
by modern leading evolutionists [18]), but is realized by the method of
"punctuated gradualism" with the recreation and reproduction of a "mosaic"
picture of development. That is why (namely — owing to such "pressure
across the whole field") any and every of the existing and developing forms
(including humanity when colliding with the so-called challenges and threats
from the side of both other forms of the surrounding world and man himself)
has to prove every moment its competitiveness, viability, and right to further
existence and development.
In the end, against the background of the general single regularity that lies
at the basis of the change-movement-development-evolution of all forms
without exception and is called the principle of self-development @, the
interference and mutual intervention of forms (in line with the same regular
striving of theirs toward the appropriation and annexation to themselves of
portions of content by taking these portions away from other forms) lead to
the chaoticness and spontaneity of the movement of individual forms, which
leads to the visible distortion of the general picture of development by
inclusions of deviations from the general regularities. The implicitly
expressed cause of the departure from the regularities of development
(determined by principle @), which (cause) is the interference-intervention of
forms in each other's spiral development, has received (both in science and
in everyday usage) the name of the all-reconciling, all-explaining, and allforgiving chance.

cosmic influences, etc.), the movement of air masses one way or another still
changes the relief of the locality (the form of the surface of segments of the
Earth). This tells on the change of the conditions of the heating of the
surface, which, in turn, influence the intensity of the heating of the air and
introduce their changes into the character of the movement of the masses of
this air. The latter again is reflected in the relief of the locality and leads to
the change of the picture of the heating of the surface. In this way, by means
of the accumulation of definite local changes (even excluding the actions of
tectonic and volcanic nature), different platforms with different climatic zones

relief of the locality) immediately begins to absorb the sun's rays in a new
way and to assimilate their energy-"content" in a new way. This is expressed
in the change of heat emission, and, consequently, in the change of the
degree and intensity of the heating of the air above the surface. The latter
circumstance, in turn, leads to the change (with respect to the preceding) of
the character of the movement of the masses of air above the same surface
and, correspondingly, to new changes of the relief (form) of the locality.
Thus, the new "form" of the surface, as it were, follows the changing heatenergy-"content" of the surrounding air medium.
Repeatedly recurring changes of this kind over a long time lead, in the final
analysis, to radical qualitative transformations — to the avalanche-like

more precisely, the "parallel-sequential" action) of the regimes of the
evaporation of water and the falling of precipitation leads at times to
substantial changes of the relief (form) of segments of the earth's surface
and to the change of climatic zones. Thus, the "form" constantly, as it were,
pulls itself up to the "content" of the changes taking place. Even without
taking into account the influence of tectonic and volcanic activity, as well as
of other cosmic influences, except the action of the sun's rays, one can see
how the content-form interaction described above is expressed in substantial
modifications of the "forms" (the change of segments of the locality, the
displacement of climatic zones), following the heat-energy-"contentful"
stratifications. From the standpoint of the described approach, by the way,
the essence of such a well-known phenomenon as the water cycle in nature,
studied in the junior grades of elementary school, is also understood more
easily.
Now let us add one more factor accompanying the process under
consideration. It is known that the formation of clouds is sometimes
accompanied by their polarization (i.e., the accumulation in the cloud of
electric charges of one sign). Upon the approach and collision (contact) of
polar-charged clouds with each other or with the earth, powerful electric
discharges (lightning) arise, which cause at times colossal changes in the
surrounding environment and lead to the appearance of new forms. Such can
happen again by reason of the change of the relief of localities because of
fires, or owing to the formation of qualitatively new chemical compounds of

elements (from the composition of the earth's crust, water, air, etc.) under
the influence of the high temperature at the place of the electric discharge.
The arisen compounds of elements, having absorbed new contentful changes,
themselves contribute to the accumulations of further content in qualitatively
new forms. It is quite possible that it is precisely such electric discharges that
led, in the opinion of scientists, to the creation of the conditions for the
emergence of the prototypes of more mobile, more flexible forms adapted to
the absorption and accumulation of subsequent changes, i.e., of "protoliving" forms and subsequently — of protoplasm. The form here unswervingly
follows the contentful changes of the surrounding space, contributing, in
turn, its own share to the volume of the contentful accumulations caused by
this movement. In the final analysis, this can lead to radical changes of a
contentful character in the form of cataclysms and the leap-like emergence of
cardinally new forms. At that, not only the outlines (relief) of the former
forms of the locality and the climatic conditions can change, but also
compounds of elements with changed and previously unseen chemical
compositions can appear, which, in turn, can become the cause of the
emergence of new more flexible and mobile contentful structures and forms.
[Let us note that the examples adduced here are examined by us under the
assumption of the absence of any elements whatsoever of living nature. The
presence of living forms would undoubtedly accelerate the content-form
transitions examined, which is what takes place in reality at the present
stage of development.
Besides, the analysis adduced here is based on the examination of only
factors belonging to the category of atmospheric phenomena. An analogous
analysis can be conducted also with the examination of the consequences of
the influence of other cyclical factors (such as, say, earthquakes with
volcanic eruptions and tsunami waves, as the rotation of the Earth around its
axis and its movement along the solar orbit, as the leaps of solar activity, as
the collisions of the Earth with celestial bodies, etc.)].
All natural cyclical phenomena (for example, the change of seasons: spring
— summer — autumn — winter) — this is nothing other than the spiral-like
ascent of the content of reality by its renewed forms, i.e., the manifestation
of the same veiled content-form movement in the direction of the
absorption of the "developing content" by the "developing content" itself.
If one speaks of the regularities, say, in the sphere of economics and social
development, then Marx's teaching on the change of socio-economic
formations owing to the development of the productive forces and the
inevitable entry of these forces into confrontation (contradiction) with the
production relations (i.e., owing to the contentful outgrowing by the
productive forces of the forms or frames of the production relations) — is
nothing other than the description of the manifestation of the action in a
concrete kind (form) and in a concrete area of reality of the same general
tendency or generalized abstract model @.

Again, the productive and any other activity of man is directed at the
creation of new forms of different levels in the expectation of the all-round
and exhaustive coverage by them and the realization (in accordance with
formulation @) of the "carrying-away" content in the image of the needs,
desires, intentions, interests, will, aspirations, goals, schemes, ideas,
theories arising in the consciousness (and subconsciousness) of man...
Billions of people daily, "every moment" consciously and unconsciously are
involved in the same single universal process of content-form
transformations and participate in the chase by their "forms" after their
own "content" fleeing from them, in the hope and striving to clothe this
"content" in the proper and befitting the current (or prospective) moment
new or renewed (perfect) "form." And let these people move (both
mechanically, and physically, and "psychophysiologically") in different
directions, commit different deeds, perform various actions (lying, sitting,
standing, on the run, moving in an automobile or airplane, etc.), the essence
of their striving, movement, existence is one and the same — the taming of
their restless, fidgety contentful "I" by clothing it in a correspondingly new
and more suitable for this new content form (both in the form of speculative
schemes and plans, and in the form of productive activity and its products,
as well as in the form of all sorts of external mechanical movements,
including the purposeful movements of various parts and organs of the
human body).
Thus, the productive (practical-theoretical) activity of modern man in
aggregate with the products of this activity (in the form of all sorts of
technologies and ideologies) represents nothing other than the perfected,
developed, complex forms of manifestation of the same single changing
reality, abiding at the levels of heightened contentfulness and having come
to replace and as a continuation of the forms of the original (simple) labor
and cognitive activity of early man in combination with its primitive primary
results.
The formulated principle of self-development is internally naturally and
organically inherent both in us (man) and in all our surroundings. The
content-form transformations of unitary directionality lying at the basis of all
real processes, described here, not only explain the spiral-cyclical character
of these processes, but also allow one to forecast their development.
One can additionally adduce a great multitude of examples interpreting the
action of the universal principle of self-development @ at different levels of
the content-form movement of reality: this internal principle ignites and
extinguishes stars, rotates the Earth, moves across the surface of the planet
the masses of soil, water, and air; it also "hides" behind the external
appearance of vortex atmospheric and oceanic currents and formations (well
distinguishable on weather images from near space), of processes of volcanic
nature, of the processes of the formation and shaping of planets, stars,
galaxies, nebulae (on photographs of far space in the overwhelming majority

of cases the patterns of spiral-like branches of the so-called interstellar and
intergalactic substance catch the eye); this same principle stands behind the
transformations of chemical elements and compounds upon the change or
loss of orbits by valence electrons at the atomic and molecular levels; it also
drives "in a circle" the herds of wild animals and flocks of migratory birds;
thanks to the realization of this principle people are born, live, communicate
with each other, embody in life their intentions and plans. Everywhere
visually and speculatively the spiral-cyclical character of really proceeding
processes is noted, behind the facade of which are hidden the content-form
transitions of known directionality.
We (humanity), equally as all our surroundings, are embraced and
simultaneously permeated by this principle, constitute its essence and
represent it in phenomena, are in its power, are subordinated to it, and it is
we who permanently renew and reproduce it. And no external act of
creation and control on the part of some "creator" or "maker" is required for
the realization of this internal principle. The world is relatively simple and
uniform in essence (by the principle of organization, the mechanism or
algorithm of the movement and development of content), but infinitely
complex and diverse in its manifestations by form — owing to the natural,
unimaginable in details mutual interlocking, interweaving, and ingrowing of
forms-levels into each other in their interaction.
Moreover, in the light of the concept expounded here, the universal and allembracing Absolute in the form of an eternal, unchanging, finished, and
perfect abstraction is unrealizable in principle, since everything existing,
in the final analysis, is a process to a much greater degree than some result
fixed once and for all, than some finished formation, than some perfect
"construction," than some "boundless-limitless essence," etc., etc. The
Universe, equally as all its separate elements, is made, recreated,
reproduced, and renewed every moment, in real-time mode, being modified,
existing, and coexisting in the form of changing forms.

"unfolds" in ever new and new contentful forms? In such a cross-section it is

forms, of the possibilities for the foretelling of the emergence of new and the
tendency of the change of existing forms, i.e., of the forecasting of coming
events and their consequences, speaking in the habitual everyday language.
The effective forecasting of the development of the forms of reality — this is
the prospect of the contentful development of humanity.

Afterword
In our days, in scientific circles there is widespread a rather biased and
arrogant attitude toward the subjective and the ideal. And meanwhile, by
what other path, if not as a result of the "construction" by individual
individuals of subjectively-abstract ideal forms, is the universally significant

and universally accepted built up in the course of successive approximations
to the reproduction of the most objective of all (i.e., the objective content of
reality)?
One should not think that everything expounded above belongs only to the
category of exercises in the construction of empty ideal abstract
constructions. Most concentratedly today the essence of even the most
pragmatic concept (theory, teaching, etc.) can be expressed only by means
of conceptual (notional) formulations, i.e., by means of abstract
subjectively-ideal forms.
The region, the medium, and, finally, the level of the ideal — this is that
"space" into which modern man lays out everything most significant, most
contentful, that he alone can dispose of today. And goals, and methods, and
schemes, and decisions, and conclusions, and models, and images, and
pictures, and plans, and programs, and ideas, and theories, and teachings,
etc., etc. — all this is from the region of the ideal (which is likewise merely a
form of the next approximation to the most complete expression of the
objective content of developing reality).
One more argument in favor of the significance of the ideal for man consists
in the fact that without an idea and, in a broader sense, outside ideology,
man simply "becomes brutish," reducing his life to the stuffing of the belly
and the performance of natural needs, i.e., to a "looped" existence at
"earthbound" levels.
It is precisely with reliance on the contentful ideal constructions being built
that the speech here is about nothing other than the prospect of the
development of humanity. In times when, behind the infinitely-routine
everyday cyclicality, man sees no meaning in life and in his existence, there
opens up the possibility of looking at all this with completely different eyes.
Every man is not simply capable, but obliged to become a researcher. In
each of us slumbers the subconscious striving to "contrive to peek around
the corner" (satisfying his innate curiosity), to foretell the onset of events, to
forecast and anticipate the appearance and development of new forms. One
needs only to activate this slumbering natural striving and impart to it a new
impulse, i.e., consciously bring it out to a new level.
The approach described here is called upon to aim man not simply at the
cognition of a certain world existing from time immemorial, but predisposes
to the meaningful investigation of everything that "is being made," that "is
being reproduced" and "recreated" directly here and now — in real-time
mode both in our near and far surroundings.
To date there exist a million concepts of the world-order. Everyone builds his
own model (picture) of the world in his own way, proceeding from the
knowledge and experience both of his own and of preceding generations.
Hence grow the most diverse variants of world-understanding, a series of

which proves to be among the dominant models of the world-order accepted
by social consciousness. Being in actual fact different approximations to one
and the same, many of them at first, and sometimes for quite a long time,
seem equivalent — go figure which variant correlates more precisely with
developing reality... But only until the time when obvious failures and
irreparable losses begin in the making of decisions and the realization of the
corresponding actions in the channel of following this or that universally
significant model. And only then, when the problem of the survival of
humanity (or at least of its part) is pushed to the foreground, does the
question of the assessment of the adequacy of the existing worldunderstanding rise to its full height, i.e., the assessment of the sufficiency
of the level (fullness) of the objective contentfulness (objective
content) of the ideal model "chosen" previously as the guiding star.
Inadequate (low-contentful) thinking (with all its low-contentful non-true
abstract subjectively-ideal forms-models of reality), having as its foundation
including false premises and sincere delusions, sooner or later risks being
punished by the premature termination of existence and the disappearance
of the very form of humanity owing to the "dragging-apart" into parts, the
absorption and assimilation of this form by more contentful competitors.
Taking into account the circumstance that there are no two or more equally
contentful and equivalent "truths" or points of view regarding one and the
same subject of judgment or investigation (i.e., there do not exist in principle
identical and equal-sized approximations to the absolute truth), one must
clearly realize that the acuteness of the question of the assessment and
choice by man of the most contentful (true) and most adequate variant of
decision from among two or more such variants will never be blunted or lost.
Meanwhile, the inadequacy of the perception of the world leads man to
catastrophe — one wants to think that someday this thought will take
possession of the developing human consciousness and become its topical
and habitual content. In such a connection it is difficult to overestimate the
significance of the decisions made by man (as well as the measure of his
responsibility) in the choice of this or that ideal model of the development of
reality. Such is the far from harmless role of every, it would seem,
insignificant subjective ideal.
Much of what has been expounded above, as has already been noted, is
presented in various sources of scientific and philosophical literature in parts.
However, such a concentrated exposition of separate views and
representations in the form of an integral single concept is reproduced for the
first time — everyone who is accustomed to systematically tracking scientific
and philosophical publications of the indicated directionality can verify this.
The ever-growing number of examples of the interpretation and confirmation
by factual material of the conceptual propositions formulated above gives
grounds to consider that the worldview concept presented here proves to be
today the most contentful and adequate.
